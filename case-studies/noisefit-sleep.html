NoiseFit sleep details page revamp

Sleep,explained.

Users could see their sleep score, but not what drove it. Naps were excluded from the calculation, sleep the band missed could not be added or corrected, and the recommended duration reflected the previous day rather than the night ahead. The redesign makes the score explainable, lets users log and edit their own sleep, and turns each night’s data into a clear plan for tonight.

Where itstarted.

the old page: every number
right, none of the reasons ↑

This is the page as it shipped: accurate numbers, presented without explanation. These are the questions people brought to it.

“Why is my score 82 and not 90?”

“I napped for an hour. Why doesn’t it count?”

“I forgot my band. Can I add last night?”

“Is ‘Recommended’ for tonight or for yesterday?”

What wasgoing wrong.

01

Naps didn’t count.

The old algorithm left naps out of the score completely. It was one of the things users complained about most.

02

There was no way to fix a night.

Sleep could only come from the band. Forgot to wear it, or slept twice? There was no way to add or edit what really happened.

03

A score without a why.

The page never showed what went into the score, so people had a number to look at and nothing to act on.

04

“Recommended” was yesterday’s number.

The 8 h 12 min next to your sleep was the previous day’s recommendation, not what you needed today.

05

No plan for tonight.

There was no improvement plan: no sleep debt, no breakdown of what you need, no bedtime to aim for.

06

It wasn’t clear what the breakdowns were for.

Breakdown listed metrics like Sleep regularity and Sleep duration, but never explained what each one measures, what a good range looks like, or how it affects the score.

Three rulesI designed by.

Explain every number.

Five contributors sit under the score, each with its value, its ideal range and a one-line explanation a tap away.

Let people fix their night.

Add or edit a sleep by hand for today, yesterday or the day before. Naps and second sleeps count towards the score.

Logging never lowers your score.

If you add a nap or a night the band missed, the day’s score can only stay the same or go up. Recording what really happened should never feel like a risk.

Same data,new story.

The score

Before A score ring with three header stats. The number carried no label, and “Recommended” showed the previous day’s figure, which was easy to misread.

After The ring now carries a status: Ideal, Good or Poor. Recommended sleep moved under Contributors, which ends the mix-up with last night’s sleep.

Score breakdown

Before Breakdown listed metrics such as Sleep regularity and Sleep duration, but never explained what each term measures, what a healthy range looks like, or how it helps users sleep better.

After Contributors sit directly under the score. Each shows its value against an ideal range, and a tap explains what the term means and why it matters.

Naps

Before Naps sat in a separate “Nap details” list and did not count towards the score.

After Naps now share one Timeline with every other sleep, in time order, and count towards the score.

Add / edit sleep

Before Sleep could only come from the band. A missed night or an unrecorded nap could not be added or corrected.

After A plus on the Timeline opens Add sleep, and anything added by hand can be edited or deleted from its menu. The next section shows the flow.

Improvement plan

Before No improvement plan. The page ended with charts and articles.

After Accumulated sleep debt, what you need tonight with its full breakdown, and a bedtime and wake-up you can turn into an alarm.

Trends

Before The earlier graphs covered only sleep duration, regularity, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and score, and only as a weekly view.

After New charts cover efficiency, hours vs needed sleep, debt, consistency and stress. A Weekly / Monthly toggle switches every chart to monthly data.

After launch

What I’llwatch.

  • Whether the redesign solves the problems users raised: do they now understand what drives their score?
  • Any error states people run into beyond the ones already designed and built.
  • Whether adding and editing sleep feels smooth from start to finish.
  • Fewer complaints about naps not counting.
  • Fewer “why did my score change?” support tickets.

What I learned

Rules before pixels.

The spec went through 12 versions. Every time a rule got clearer, a screen got simpler.

An edge case is someone’s Tuesday.

Naps, late nights and a forgotten band each got a screen, not a footnote.

Say no kindly.

“Daily limit of 4 sleeps reached.” Every blocked action tells you why, and what you can do instead.
